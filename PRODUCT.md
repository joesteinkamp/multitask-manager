# Product

> Generated by `project-starter-pack`. This is the source of truth for **who this product is for, what it is, and why it exists**. Every other brief (design, technical) and every AI agent reading this repo should anchor here first.

## Register

product — a daily-use tool, opened dozens of times a day for a few seconds at a time. Earned familiarity beats novelty; the interface should disappear into the work rather than announce itself. If reaching it costs a context switch, it has already failed.

## One-liner

MultiTask Manager keeps you on top of every project you're running in parallel with your AI agents — what's happening, what needs you, and what to do next.

## Users

### Primary

One person running four to six projects at once, most of the work split between themselves and AI agents. A designer or builder, senior enough to be the one deciding what gets built, working across Claude Code, Codex and Cursor simultaneously — often several sessions live in different repos at the same time. They are the bottleneck in their own system: agents block on them constantly, for an approval, a question, or just a finished run nobody collected, and they find out minutes or hours later by tabbing through terminals.

What they tried before: `agent watch`, which tracked live sessions and helped — but only sessions, which is one layer below the thing they actually manage. Before that, terminal tabs and memory. Team trackers were never a candidate; the ceremony costs more than the work.

### Secondary

**The agents themselves.** Through the MCP server, an agent is a genuine consumer of this product — it reads the board, takes the next task, reports progress, and files new work it discovered elsewhere. Any feature that only makes sense for a human reader is probably modelling the wrong thing.

## Product Purpose

AI turned working on one thing into working on five. Each project now has agents doing parts of it and a person doing others, and the state of all of it is scattered across terminals, repos, and whatever tracker the project actually lives in. **Staying on top of the work has become harder than the work.**

That is a multitasking problem, not an agent problem, and a better agent doesn't solve it. It gets solved by something that holds the whole picture and is always within reach.

Most people feeling this haven't named it yet — they assume the scattered mess is simply what running several agents looks like. So this doesn't teach them the problem exists; it shows them the state of their own work in one place and lets the relief make the argument.

The destination: agents answer their own prompts and move to the next task themselves, with this app handing them that work and escalating to the human only when a decision genuinely needs one.

### Success metrics

- **Time from quiet to noticed** — median minutes between a session needing the user and the user knowing. This is the core promise, and it is measurable against the audit log.
- **Projects that go dormant unnoticed** — target zero. A stuck project makes noise; a forgotten one goes silent, and silence reads exactly like fine. This is the specific failure a multi-project week produces.

## Brand Personality

**Three words:** direct, calm, precise

It says the thing and doesn't dramatise it. A notification reads "Needs approval — Bash(rm -rf build/)", never "⚠️ Something needs your attention!" — the exact session, the exact reason, no exclamation mark. Calm is load-bearing rather than stylistic: this is a tool that interrupts you, so every interruption has to be worth the cost of the interruption, and the copy should lower the pulse rather than raise it. Precise means naming the real thing — a real file path, a real branch, a real count — and saying plainly when it doesn't know: "No PRODUCT.md — add one to get suggestions" beats a blank panel every time.

## Anti-references

- **Gamified to-do apps** (Todoist streaks, Habitica, confetti on completion) — manufacturing urgency about your own work is the opposite of the point. No streaks, no badges, no celebration of a finished task.
- **"AI copilot" dashboards** — purple gradients, chat-bubble heroes, "summarize anything in seconds". The generic AI-product aesthetic, which promises intelligence and delivers a text box.
- **Activity Monitor and raw log viewers** — data without judgment. A wall of rows that leaves you to work out what matters is the failure mode this product is closest to and must stay furthest from: every surface takes a position on what deserves attention.

## Design Principles

<!-- Lifted from ROADMAP.md, which stated these first. -->

- **At your fingertips, or it has failed.** An ambient tool that costs a context switch to reach doesn't get reached. The popover and the global hotkey are the product, not the delivery mechanism.
- **Two kinds of actor.** Every piece of work is assignable to the user or to an agent. A feature that only makes sense for one of the two is usually a wrong abstraction.
- **Say why, not just what.** Every status carries the reason it was chosen. A verdict you can't interrogate is one you stop trusting the first time it surprises you.
- **Degrade gracefully.** Every signal is optional. A missing harness, missing hooks, or a renamed upstream path means fewer features — never a broken app.
- **Observe before you control.** A capability ships read-only first and gains writes only after the read-only version has been in daily use.
- **No inference in the briefing path.** Goal, Now and Next stay pure file reads — fast, private, and correct. Where reasoning is genuinely needed, dispatch a delegate and read its output; the app itself never calls a model.

## Accessibility & Inclusion commitments

**WCAG 2.2 AA.** Specifically, for a surface this small and this fast: fully keyboard-operable once open (arrows move, Enter activates, Esc closes), a visible focus state at all times, 24px minimum target size, and `prefers-reduced-motion` honoured for any transition. Status is never carried by colour alone — every state has a label and a written reason beside its dot. Notification copy is legible on its own, without the app open.

## Anti-patterns (always reject)

<!-- Embedded from guardrails/product-anti-patterns.md. -->

1. **Vague personas.** "Developers who want better tools" describes ten million people and helps nobody decide anything.
2. **Generic positioning.** If the same sentence could appear on a competitor's site, it isn't positioning.
3. **Education framing for an aware audience.** If users already know they have the problem, solve it — don't teach it.
4. **Hedging language in brand voice.** "Might", "could", "consider", "perhaps". A brand that hedges trains its AI to hedge in the UI.
5. **Feature lists as positioning.** Lead with the promise, support with proof.
6. **Ornamental success metrics.** "Increased engagement" is not a metric. Measure what you'd be embarrassed to miss.
7. **Anti-references that aren't real.** "Not boring" constrains nothing. Name actual products and actual aesthetics.
8. **Brand personality of three buzzwords.** "Modern, clean, friendly" describes 90% of products. It should be recognisable in a paragraph with the logo removed.
